What Types of QR Codes Can You Create?

QRCraft supports five QR code types, each encoding different kinds of information:

  • URL QR Code — Encodes a website address. When scanned, opens the URL in the phone's browser. Most common use case for businesses, marketing and product packaging.
  • WiFi QR Code — Encodes a WiFi network name (SSID), password and security type (WPA/WEP/open). Guests scan it to connect instantly without typing the password.
  • vCard / Contact QR Code — Encodes name, phone, email and address in vCard 3.0 format. Scanning adds the contact directly to the phone's address book.
  • Text QR Code — Encodes plain text. Useful for displaying short messages, serial numbers or codes.
  • SMS QR Code — Encodes a phone number and pre-written SMS message. Opens the SMS app with the message pre-filled.

QR Code Best Practices

Follow these guidelines to ensure your QR codes are always scannable:

  • Minimum size: Print QR codes at least 2 cm × 2 cm (about 0.8 inches). Smaller codes are harder to scan from a distance.
  • Quiet zone: Leave a white margin of at least 4 modules (the small squares) around all four sides of the QR code.
  • Contrast: Use dark modules on a light background. Never invert this — light-on-dark codes often fail on some scanners.
  • Short URLs: Shorter URLs produce less dense QR codes that scan more reliably. Use a URL shortener for very long links.
  • Test before printing: Always scan your QR code with at least two different phones before committing to a print run.
  • Use SVG for print: SVG files scale to any size without pixelation. Use PNG only for screen/web.

WiFi QR Code — What to Enter

For WiFi QR codes, you need three pieces of information:

  • SSID: Your network name exactly as it appears in WiFi settings (case-sensitive).
  • Password: Your WiFi password. If your network has no password, leave this blank and select "None" for security type.
  • Security type: WPA/WPA2 (most home routers), WEP (older/insecure), or None (open network).

The resulting QR code, when scanned on Android 10+ or iOS 11+, prompts the user to join the network automatically.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a QR code?

A QR (Quick Response) code is a two-dimensional barcode that smartphones scan to instantly open URLs, connect to WiFi, save contacts, or display text. Invented by Denso Wave in 1994, they are now natively supported by all modern phone cameras.

Can I customise QR code colours?

Yes. You can change the foreground (module) colour and background colour. Keep sufficient contrast — dark modules on light background — so scanners reliably read it. Avoid reversing this or using similar colours for both.

Do QR codes expire?

Static QR codes that encode data directly (like a URL) never expire on their own. However, if the destination URL goes offline or changes, the QR code will lead to a broken link. Always test periodically and use stable URLs for printed materials.
